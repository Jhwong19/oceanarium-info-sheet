Section One

In the Beginning

Life started in water! Step into a time machine — meet plankton, sea jellies, and ancient sea creatures from millions of years ago.

Tiny Worlds, Ancient Wonders 🌊

A single drop of seawater can be packed with plankton — the tiny living things that feed almost everything in the ocean!

Sea jellies have been drifting around for over 500 million years — that's older than the dinosaurs! 🦖

🔭 Look out for…

  • Drop of Water — see plankton blown up huge so you can spot the hidden world inside one tiny drop.
  • Ocean Wonders — Moon Jellies and Fried Egg Sea Jellies floating like spaceships.
  • Ancient Waters — giant Dunkleosteus and Xiphactinus replicas, fossils and animatronics!
  • Conquering Land — Axolotls and frogs that show how some animals moved from sea to shore.

⭐ Fun facts

  • Most ocean food chains begin with plankton.
  • Sea jellies have NO bones — they're more than 95% water!
  • Some ancient fish wore armor like a knight.
  • Amphibians help us understand how animals first walked on land.

Section Two

At the Surface

Where land meets sea! Singapore's coast is full of mudskippers walking on mud, archerfish shooting water, and mangroves that act like a nursery for baby sea animals.

Singapore's Coast 🌳

The edge of the sea is busy with life! Mangroves are like baby nurseries — small fish and crabs grow up safely between their tangled roots.

Step aboard the Jewel of Muscat, a cool replica of an ancient sailing ship. Imagine crossing oceans long before GPS! 🧭

🔭 Look out for…

  • Spirit of Exploration — the Jewel of Muscat ship from long-ago sea voyages.
  • Singapore's Coast — our local mangrove home, with muddy roots and salty water.
  • Local heroes — Archerfish, Barred Mudskippers, Knobbly Sea Stars and Spotted Seahorses.
  • Projection magic — coastal animals that come alive on the walls!

⭐ Fun facts

  • Mangroves are nurseries for young marine animals.
  • Mudskippers can walk on mud using their fins!
  • Archerfish shoot water like tiny water-pistols to knock bugs off leaves.
  • Singapore is busy and full of buildings, but our coasts still hide cool creatures.

🧠 Mini quiz (tap to reveal)

What kind of coastal forest grows where land and sea meet?
→ Mangroves!
Which fish can shoot water to catch prey?
→ The Archerfish
Why are mangroves important for young animals?
→ They give shelter and a safe place to grow.

Section Three

Sunlight

The bright, busy ocean! Sharks glide through tunnels, manta rays soar like underwater birds, and corals build whole cities for thousands of fish. 🦈

A City of the Sea 🪸

Coral reefs are sometimes called cities of the sea — busy underwater neighbourhoods full of fish, clams and even sharks!

Watch manta rays "fly" through the Open Ocean tank. They look like underwater birds with giant wings.

🔭 Look out for…

  • Shark Tunnel — Sand Tigers, Scalloped Hammerheads, Sandbar Sharks gliding above your head.
  • Coral Gardens — colourful corals and giant clams that filter ocean water.
  • Reef + Kelp Forest — Weedy Seadragons, Dog Face Puffers, swaying kelp.
  • Open Ocean — Manta Rays, Spotted Eagle Rays and Zebra Sharks.

⭐ Fun facts

  • Sharks have NO bones — their skeletons are made of bendy cartilage (like your nose!).
  • Coral reefs are sometimes called "cities of the sea".
  • Manta rays look like they're flying underwater.
  • Kelp can grow tall enough to make underwater forests!

🧠 Mini quiz (tap to reveal)

→ Sharks!
Are corals rocks, plants, or animals?
→ Animals — tiny ones living together!
What animal looks like it is flying underwater?
→ Manta rays

Section Four

Into the Deep

Down we go! Discover ocean currents like giant rivers in the sea, dolphins on epic journeys, and the wiggly creatures of the seafloor. 🐬

Underwater Highways 🌊

Ocean currents are like giant rivers inside the sea — they carry food, animals and (sadly) even rubbish across the world.

Some animals migrate thousands of kilometres every year. Imagine swimming from Singapore to Australia and back!

🔭 Look out for…

  • Open Ocean Currents — including a "Garbage Patch" exhibit about plastic pollution.
  • Migrators — Japanese Pineapplefish and Indo-Pacific Bottlenose Dolphins.
  • Benthos — life on the seafloor: Green Morays, Tasmanian Giant Crabs, Banggai Cardinalfish, Yellow Tang.
  • Artificial Habitats — shipwrecks turned into homes for sea life.

⭐ Fun facts

  • Some sea animals migrate further than humans drive cars in a year.
  • The ocean floor is full of hidden life — it's not empty at all!
  • Sunken ships can become busy underwater apartments.
  • Plastic thrown on land can travel all the way to the deep sea. 😢

🧠 Mini quiz (tap to reveal)

What are ocean currents like?
→ Moving rivers in the sea!
What does "benthos" mean?
→ Life on or near the ocean floor.
How can a shipwreck help marine animals?
→ It can become a shelter or artificial reef.

Section Five

The Abyss

Welcome to the dark deep sea! No sunlight, huge pressure, and creatures that make their own light. 🔦✨

Glow in the Dark ✨

Some animals here make their own light — that's called bioluminescence. They use it to find food, hide from danger, or send messages to friends.

When a whale dies, its body sinks and feeds deep-sea creatures for YEARS. That's called a whale fall.

🔭 Look out for…

  • Deep Ocean Exploration — submersibles humans use to dive where we can't swim.
  • Life in the Deep — anglerfish, glowing creatures and other shadowy hunters.
  • Whale Fall + Seamount — a real whale skeleton with magical projections.
  • Trenches — some are deeper than Mount Everest is tall! 🏔️

⭐ Fun facts

  • The deep sea is darker than the darkest night.
  • Some animals make their own light — it's called bioluminescence.
  • A whale fall feeds deep-sea life for many years.
  • We've explored more of the Moon than the deepest ocean!

🧠 Mini quiz (tap to reveal)

→ Bioluminescence
What is a whale fall?
→ A dead whale that sinks and feeds deep-sea life.

→ Very deep — deeper than any mountain is tall!

Section Six

A New Horizon

Protecting our blue planet! The ocean's future is in our hands — and even small actions can make a HUGE difference. 💙🌍

Hope for our Ocean 💙

The ocean helps control Earth's climate — but warming seas, melting ice and pollution are making things harder for sea life.

The good news? Every kid is a hero in training! Tiny actions ripple out — just like waves.

🔭 Look out for…

  • Ocean's Future — an immersive illusion of melting ice and rising seas.
  • Lionfish habitat — beautiful, but an invasive species in some places.
  • Hallway of Hope — stories of conservation heroes and what YOU can do.

⭐ Fun facts

  • The ocean helps control Earth's climate.
  • Rising seas can affect islands and coastal cities — like Singapore!
  • Invasive species are animals that move where they don't belong.
  • Using less plastic = a happier ocean. 🐢

🧠 Mini quiz (tap to reveal)

What can happen when ice melts and sea levels rise?
→ Coastal places may flood or change shape.
What is an invasive species?
→ A species that spreads where it does not naturally belong.
Name one thing kids can do to help the ocean.
→ Use less plastic, recycle, never litter, learn more!
